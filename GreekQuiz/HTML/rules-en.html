Articles and word endings by gender and number

Definite articles (Οριστικά άρθρα)

Gender / Number Nominative Accusative Genitive
Masculine (sg.)οτοντου
Feminine (sg.)ητη(ν)της
Neuter (sg.)τοτοτου
Masculine (pl.)οιτουςτων
Feminine (pl.)οιτιςτων
Neuter (pl.)τατατων

Definite articles are used when talking about something specific or already known. For example, "το σπίτι" (the house we both know about), "ο ήλιος" (the sun, one of a kind), "η Ελλάδα" (Greece, a specific country).

Indefinite articles (Αόριστα άρθρα)

Gender Indefinite article
Masculineένας
Feminineμία / μια
Neuterένα

Indefinite articles are used when talking about something non-specific, someone or something mentioned for the first time. For example, "ένα βιβλίο" (a book, any book), "μία γυναίκα" (a woman), "ένας άντρας" (a man).

Typical noun endings (Ενδεικτικές καταλήξεις ουσιαστικών)

There are many exceptions and declensions in Greek. These are just general examples.

Gender Endings Example

Possessive pronouns / adjectives (Κτητικές αντωνυμίες / επίθετα)

In Greek, possessive adjectives are declined by gender, number, and case. The most common construction is article + noun + personal pronoun in the genitive case

English Construction (example: "my book" - το βιβλίο)
Myτο βιβλίο μου (my book)
Your (sg.)το βιβλίο σου (your book)
His/Her/Itsτο βιβλίο του (his book) / το βιβλίο της (her book)
Ourτο βιβλίο μας (our book)
Your (pl.)το βιβλίο σας (your book)
Theirτο βιβλίο τους (their book)

You can also use the words δικός/δική/δικό + pronoun in the genitive case to emphasize possession (for example: το δικό μου βιβλίο - my *own* book).

How to say "no" (Negation)

To negate verbs and phrases, the particles δεν (before a verb) and μη(ν) (in the imperative mood, for prohibitions) are used. Όχι is used as a separate word for "no".

  • Δεν (to negate verbs in the indicative mood):
    • Εγώ δεν μιλάω. (I do not speak.)
    • Αυτός δεν είναι Έλληνας. (He is not Greek.)
  • Μην (for prohibitions and negations in the imperative/subjunctive mood):
    • Μην μιλάς! (Don't speak!)
    • Μην το κάνεις! (Don't do it!)
  • Όχι (as the answer "no" or to negate nouns):
    • - Είσαι Έλληνας; - Όχι (- Are you Greek? - No.)
    • Όχι καφές, τσάй. (Not coffee, tea.)

Sentence and question structure

Affirmative sentence structure (Δομή καταφατικής πρότασης)

Subject (Υποκείμενο) + Verb (Ρήμα) + Object/Complement (Αντικείμενο/Συμπλήρωμα)

  • Εγώ διαβάζω ένα βιβλίο (I am reading a book.)
  • Ο Γιάννης τρέχει γρήγορα (Yiannis runs quickly.)

Interrogative sentence structure (Δομή ερωτηματικής πρότασης)

A question can be asked by:

1. Intonation (Τονισμός)

The most common way in spoken language. The intonation rises at the end of the sentence.

  • Διαβάζεις ένα βιβλίο; (Are you reading a book?)

2. Using question words (Χρήση ερωτηματικών λέξεων)

The question word is usually placed at the beginning of the sentence.

  • Πού μένεις; (Where do you live?)
  • Τι κάνεις; (What are you doing?)

Other verb tenses

Past tense (Αόριστος)

The past tense (Αόριστος) denotes a single, completed action in the past. It is used with time markers like "χθες" (yesterday), "πριν" (before), "προχθές" (the day before yesterday).

PronounVerb "γράφω" (to write)
Εγώέγραψα
Εσύέγραψες
Αυτός/Αυτή/Αυτόέγραψε
Εμείςγράψαμε
Εσείςγράψατε
Αυτοί/Αυτές/Αυτάέγραψαν

Future tense (Μέλλοντας)

The future tense (Μέλλοντας) denotes an action that will happen in the future. It is formed with the particle "θα" + verb in the present tense (ενεστώτας) or with a special form depending on the verb (simple or continuous future).

PronounVerb "πάω" (to go)
Εγώθα πάω
Εσύθα πας
Αυτός/Αυτή/Αυτόθα πάει
Εμείςθα πάμε
Εσείςθα πάτε
Αυτοί/Αυτές/Αυτάθα πάνε

Types of future tense

  • Μέλλοντας στιγμιαίος (simple future) — used for single, completed actions in the future. Example: θα γράψω (I will write).
  • Μέλλοντας εξακολουθητικός (continuous future) — for actions that will take place over a period of time. Example: θα γράφω (I will be writing).

Comparison: γράφω

TenseFormExample
PresentγράφωΤώρα γράφω ένα γράμμα. (Now I am writing a letter.)
Future (cont.)θα γράφωΑύριο θα γράφω όλη μέρα. (Tomorrow I will be writing all day.)
Future (simple)θα γράψωΘα γράψω ένα βιβλίο. (I will write a book.)
